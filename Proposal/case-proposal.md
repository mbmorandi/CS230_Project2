## MGM Studios v. Grokster   

Grokster and Streamcast, former technology companies, distributed free peer-to-peer software that allowed users to share files.    
With this technology, user bases shared music and videos, violating copyright laws.    
In 2004, MGM Studios sued both companies, arguing that their software encouraged copyright infringement.    
In 2005, the Supreme Court ruled in favor of MGM Studios, stating that if a company encourages infringement, they can be held liable,    
establishing the inducement theory of copyright liability. MGM Studios v. Grokster is foundational to the ethical examination of copyright and   
intellectual property. Furthermore, MGM Studios v. Grokster beckons us to question the level of responsibility a company should have on behalf of its users.
