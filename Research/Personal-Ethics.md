What is MGM and Grokster?

Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer is an American Media production company specializing in the distribution of movies and tv programs. 

Grokster is a peer to peer file sharing software that allows users to easily upload and download files without restrictions. 



How Grokster worked was it connected a user directly to another user's computer. Allowing them to have easy access to the other party's files. 

Since Grokster was free, easy to use, and didn't have many restrictions, it was the perfect software for distributing pirated content. All someone had to do was boot up Grokster, search for a piece of media and it would connect them to someone in the network who has that media.

Piracy is illegal for a reason. By pirating content online, you are essentially stealing someone's work and putting it out for free. By pirating media, you are not just getting that piece of media for free but rather, you open a door for others to get it without the original producer getting compensated. It takes one person to pirate a piece of media for the producer to lose out on millions of dollars. Since media production is time consuming and expensive, producers are depending on the work to generate revenue. By allowing people to copy and distribute that piece of media to others, revenue is sure to decline. Declining revenue forces media producers out of business, or increases the price of which consumers have to pay for that piece of media. Either way, it hurts both the people involved in the creation of that media and the legitimate consumers who buy into it.

Should Grokster be involved in preventing users from sharing copyrighted content? 

This is a great ethical concern but first we have to understand what Grokster is designed for. Grokster’s technology is what we'd call a dual-use tech. The term Dual-use is used to describe technology that is unrestricted and can be used for both good or bad. For example, guns would be considered a dual-use innovation. On one hand, it protects people, on the other it harms people. Grokster’s technological framework is meant to be quick, easy to use, and unrestricted. It does not care what files you're sharing. This is great because there is nothing spent on adding these restrictions, it allows anyone to share whatever they want, it doesn't waste time checking if the file met the restrictions and more importantly, it gives users freedom. This is Grokster's main selling point, and adding restrictions attracts significantly less users. However, pirated content isn't the only issue when it comes to unregulated file sharing. It can also be used to distribute viruses, illegal documents, NSFW content, and more. And more importantly, should unlimited freedom be prioritized over the possibility of ruin?
